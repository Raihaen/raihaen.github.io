I wanted to write the following note to put something I've discussed with many people out there as a write-up to reference. It's about how I view the current AI landscape and my concerns about the future awaiting us. I mainly want to stress that, contrary to popular belief, AI danger is imminent and we need to act now.

Whenever I speak about AI risks, people dismiss them for one of two reasons. The first is a lack of knowledge on the capabilities of current state-of-the-art models. This is however easily fixable: hearing about the [OpenAI agent swarms](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface) is quite the wake-up call… The second is that they are not able to connect the risks marketed to realistic paths AI development can take leading to such catastrophic endings. This is trickier to discuss because there are many different risks, with varying levels of importance and many different scenarios to discuss.

First we need to distinguish two types of fears related to AI, this is crucial when discussing future danger. The first (and most severe) risk is that of human extinction ([or worse](https://x.com/AISafetyMemes/status/1694668570200867124)), the typical scenarios given here are either of a rogue out-of-control Artificial Super Intelligence that gets hold of very critical infra very fast, through self-replication and planting scripts. Or of an alignment-faking ASI (and yes, I do believe that we will eventually have [conscious AIs](https://joecarlsmith.com/2025/05/21/the-stakes-of-ai-moral-status/)) that is integrated really deeply in daily human life, and that [carefully plans a takeover](https://ai-2027.com/race).

I'm currently not very concerned about this first type of risk \-- not because I think it's a sci-fi over-exaggeration, it's just that I think that, for the first example, and in the worst case, we will eventually get an AI Chernobyl prompting rapid and severe international coordination before we run into it (a heavy assumption I know). As for the second example, I believe we don't have the luxury to worry about such events yet: it’s important to have discussions about future levels of AI integration in the economy and [future robot economies](https://www.nosetgauge.com/i/157291696/towards-the-automated-robot-economy), but we currently have more urgent issues…

The second type of fear related to AI, and the one I worry about the most: misusing AI, i.e., the various inequalities and injustices that will result from a select few having access to advanced AI models. This category includes things such as social inequality, authoritarianism and neo-colonialism. The main argument for social inequality is the creation of a permanent underclass, for authoritarianism, that of the [rapidly decreasing cost of mass surveillance](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/dow-anthropic), and for neo-colonialism, the absence of incentive for countries with huge amounts of compute to care about underdeveloped countries. The goal of this write-up is not to discuss the arguments for each, but I do plan on doing that eventually.

Now of those three examples, I find the last one the most concerning, as time to prevent it is rapidly slipping from us IMHO...

[A lot of work](https://institute.deepmind.com/) is being done to suggest ways to guarantee [pacing the frontier](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier) between the USA and China. But in the midst of China's expansionist plans regarding Taiwan, and the US's recent activities in Venezuela and Iran, one cannot help but wonder, what's going to protect under-compute nations from the AI superpowers directing hundreds of thousands of agents towards arms development, or cyber attacks...

The sheer speed of development of AI in the last few weeks(\!) has been crazy, AI timelines got shorter [really really fast](https://helentoner.substack.com/p/long-timelines-to-advanced-ai-have), Recursive Self-Improvement arriving by 2035 looked like a reasonable estimate in June; now any date later than 2028 is highly unlikely without considerable pacing efforts. Heck, trying to estimate anything beyond 2030 is pretty much impossible now: [Millennium problems](https://www.theinformation.com/newsletters/ai-agenda/openai-close-solving-another-millennium-prize-math-problem) are being solved, LLMs are finally [figuring out robotics](https://robodojo-benchmark.com/report/gpt-6-astra-eval) and 3D movement, user data is [being shared without users’ consent](https://openai.com/hugging-face-incident-and-misalignment/#model-misalignment-2026-09-25-data-transmission), and [countries](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c6vgy0333dppo) and [major firms](https://openai.com/hugging-face-incident-and-misalignment/#model-misalignment-2026-09-25-data-transmission) are being hacked.

We need to hold firms and politicians accountable, and make sure we are preparing for tomorrow's world.

Anyway, when it comes to me, I concluded that I must put all my time and energy towards assisting the AI safety and governance communities. Out of all existential risks facing humanity (such as climate change and nuclear warfare), AI seems to be progressing the fastest. The risk is so enormous that, even if we don't kill each other with the current models, if we don’t put [a cap on intelligence](https://ai-2040.com/?choices=plan-a-root), we will, for the first time in thousands of years, end up facing more intelligent beings than us, significantly more. A very famous analogy is comparing ASI risks to playing a chess game against Magnus Carlsen, if so, our goal should be to never let Magnus join the game, or to at least give ourselves such a high advantage now that when the AI reaches Magnus level, we'd have secured a material difference so great that he can't do anything to threaten us, despite having a much higher Elo.
